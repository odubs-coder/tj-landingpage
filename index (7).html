The problem
Your journal doesn't speak your language.

Every journal on the market wants your ticker and your win rate. None of them track what confluences work for you, your most profitable killzone, or even when you break your rules.

So you tag everything "setup A" and "setup B", and six months later you still can't answer the only question worth asking — which of your confluences are earning, and which ones you just say out loud.

You remember your winners. You quietly forget the losers on the same setup. Only a log fixes that.

What it does differently

Three things no other journal gives you.

Ranks your confluences

Tag what you actually saw. After 30 trades it shows the average R behind each one, including the ones quietly costing you money.

Prices every broken rule

Write your rules, flag them when you break them. It totals what each one has cost you in pounds and in R. "No revenge trades" stops being a slogan.

Runs on your session

Asia, London open, post-London, pre-NY, NY AM, NY PM. Log at 00:30 and it lands on the right day, because it was built for this session.

On purpose

No broker connection.

You type your trades in. It takes thirty seconds, and that's the point — writing down what you saw is the review. Auto-import gives you a database. This gives you a journal.

It also means no broker permissions, no account to breach, and no company sitting on your P&L. Your data stays on your device, and you can export all of it in one tap.
